# רבי יעקב עמדין

**Rabbi Yaakov Emden** (1697–1776), widely known by the acronym **Ya'avetz** (יעב"ץ), was a prominent German rabbi, Talmudist, and a zealous defender of traditional Judaism. He was the son of the Hakham Zvi (Rabbi Zvi Ashkenazi). 

### Background
- **Sabbatean Controversy**: The Ya'avetz is perhaps most historically famous for his fierce and lifelong campaign to eradicate the remnants of the Sabbatean (Shabbetaian) movement. This culminated in the highly publicized and divisive Emden-Eybeschütz controversy, in which he accused the renowned Rabbi Yehonatan Eybeschütz of secretly harboring Sabbatean beliefs.
- **Independence**: A fiercely independent thinker, he largely avoided holding official rabbinic posts for most of his life to maintain his intellectual and religious freedom. Instead, he operated a private printing press in Altona (near Hamburg) which he used to publish his own works.
- **Critical Scholarship**: He was known for his critical approach to texts. Most famously, in his work *Mitpachat Sefarim*, he boldly questioned the antiquity of parts of the Zohar, suggesting they were later additions.

### Major Works
- **Siddur Ya'avetz (Amudei Shamayim)**: His comprehensive commentary and arrangement of the prayer book, still widely respected and studied for its halachic depth and exactitude.
- **She'eilat Ya'avetz** (שאילת יעב"ץ): His primary collection of Halachic responsa.
- **Mor U'Ketzia** (מור וקציעה): A profound commentary on the Shulchan Aruch (Orach Chayim).
- **Lechem Shamayim** (לחם שמים): His commentary on the Mishnah.
- **Megillat Sefer** (מגילת ספר): His fascinating autobiography, providing a rare and candid look into his life and the era.
