# הרב יוסף דוב סולוביצ׳יק

## Background Info
- **Lifespan**: 1903–1993 (ה'תרס"ג–ה'תשנ"ג)
- **Lineage & Background**: Born in Pruzhany, Belarus, into the renowned Brisker rabbinic dynasty. Son of Rabbi Moshe Soloveichik and grandson of Rabbi Chaim Soloveitchik (Reb Chaim Brisker, רבי חיים מבריסק), named after his great-grandfather the Beit HaLevi (Rabbi Yosef Dov Soloveitchik). He was trained directly by his father in the conceptual Brisker method of Talmudic analysis (*derekh Brisk*). He later earned a Ph.D. in philosophy at the University of Berlin in 1932.
- **Rabbinic Leadership**: Immigrated to the United States in 1932, serving as Chief Rabbi of Boston where he founded the Maimonides School. In 1941, he succeeded his father as Rosh Yeshiva of Rabbi Isaac Elchanan Theological Seminary (RIETS) at Yeshiva University in New York, a position he held for over four decades, ordaining more than 2,000 rabbis and serving as the primary intellectual and halakhic guide for American Modern Orthodoxy and the Rabbinical Council of America (RCA).
- **Thought & Legacy**: Renowned for synthesizing rigorous Lithuanian Brisker lamdanus with existential philosophy and theological depth. His seminal works include *Ish HaHalakha* (*איש ההלכה* / *Halakhic Man*), *The Lonely Man of Faith*, *Al HaTeshuvah* (*על התשובה*), *Kol Dodi Dofek*, along with transcriptions of his extensive Talmudic and Yahrtzeit shiurim, including *Shiurim LeZekher Abba Mari* (*שיעורים לזכר אבא מרי*), *MiBeit Midrasho Shel HaRav* (*מבית מדרשו של הרב*), and *Reshimot Shiurim*.
